🌍Station 2: Volcano hotspots

Many volcanoes form near the edges of tectonic plates. The Pacific Ring of Fire is a huge zone of earthquakes and volcanoes around the Pacific Ocean.

🇳🇿 New Zealand

Volcanic activity is linked to the boundary between the Pacific and Australian plates. The Taupō Volcanic Zone includes active volcanoes and geothermal features.

🇯🇵 Japan

Japan sits where several tectonic plates meet. It has many volcanoes, including Mount Fuji.

🇺🇸 Hawaii

The Hawaiian islands formed over a hotspot in the middle of the Pacific Plate, rather than at a plate boundary.

⛰️Station 3: Volcano shape-shifters

Not all volcanoes look or erupt in the same way. Match each volcano type to its description.

🛡️ Shield volcano

Wide with gentle slopes. Usually built by runny lava that can travel a long way.

🍦 Cinder cone

Usually small and steep-sided, made from loose volcanic fragments that pile up around a vent.

🏔️ Composite volcano

Tall, often steep-sided volcano built from layers of lava and ash/other volcanic material.

Need a hint?Think about the shape: a shield is broad and low; a cinder cone is small and steep; a composite volcano is built in layers.

🔥Station 4: Eruption investigation

Magma is molten rock beneath Earth's surface. When it reaches the surface, it is called lava. The amount of gas and how easily magma flows help influence how explosive an eruption can be.

🌊 Runny magma

Low viscosity (flows easily). Gas can often escape more easily, so eruptions may be gentler and produce lava flows.

🍯 Sticky magma

High viscosity (flows slowly). Gas can become trapped, pressure can build and eruptions may be explosive.
